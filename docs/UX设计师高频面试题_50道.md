# UX 设计师高频面试题 50 道（含跨角色与 AI 方向）

适用对象：产品设计师、UX/UI 设计师、交互设计师。题目按常见面试官角色整理；同一道主问题通常会被连续追问，准备时建议为每题配一个真实项目案例。

## 一、HR / 招聘方：动机、经历与职业选择（1–8）

1. 请用 3 分钟介绍一下你自己，以及最能代表你设计能力的经历。
2. 为什么选择 UX / 产品设计，而不是纯视觉设计或产品经理？
3. 你为什么想离开现在（或上一份）工作？
4. 为什么选择我们公司、这个产品和这个岗位？
5. 你的职业规划是什么？未来 3 年希望成长为怎样的设计师？
6. 你认为自己最突出的优势是什么？它如何在项目中产生价值？
7. 说一个你做得不够好的项目或决定；后来你怎样改进？
8. 当工作节奏快、需求频繁变化时，你如何管理压力和优先级？

## 二、设计负责人 / 资深设计师：项目深挖与专业判断（9–20）

9. 请完整讲一个你主导的项目：背景、目标、过程、结果和你的具体贡献分别是什么？
10. 在这个项目里，最初的问题是如何定义的？你如何确认它值得解决？
11. 你做了哪些用户研究？研究结论如何真正改变了设计决策？
12. 当用户需求、业务目标和技术成本冲突时，你如何做取舍？
13. 请讲一次你推翻自己初始设计方案的经历，是什么证据让你改变了判断？
14. 你如何为复杂流程建立信息架构和任务流？
15. 你怎样区分“用户说想要的”与“用户真正需要的”？
16. 面对模糊需求，你通常从哪里开始推进？
17. 你如何判断一个界面是清晰、易用且有层级的？
18. 讲一个你用数据衡量设计效果的案例：指标是什么、如何归因、结果如何？
19. 当可用性测试或线上数据不支持你的方案时，你会怎么做？
20. 你如何把一个一次性的设计方案沉淀为组件、规范或设计系统？

## 三、产品经理：业务理解、策略与协作（21–29）

21. 如果 PM 只给你一句“把转化率做高”，你会如何拆解问题并开始设计？
22. 你如何理解这个产品当前最关键的用户价值和商业目标？
23. 请讲一次你主动发现机会、而不是接到需求后才开始设计的经历。
24. 当你不同意 PM 的需求优先级或方案时，你如何表达并推动决策？
25. 你如何将研究洞察转化成可执行、可验证的产品假设？
26. 你会如何设计并评估一个新功能从 0 到 1 的 MVP？
27. 面对短期转化目标与长期用户体验，你会如何选择？
28. 你如何参与需求评审，避免团队做出“看似合理但不解决问题”的功能？
29. 如果上线后核心指标没有改善，你会优先排查哪些可能性？

## 四、工程师 / 技术负责人：可实现性与研发协作（30–36）

30. 你会在什么阶段邀请工程师参与设计？为什么？
31. 请讲一次因技术限制而改方案的经历：你如何找到体验与成本之间的平衡？
32. 你如何向工程师交付设计，确保实现效果和设计意图一致？
33. 当研发认为方案过于复杂、排期不可接受时，你会如何拆分或降级？
34. 你对前端、平台能力、数据埋点或无障碍分别了解多少？这些知识如何帮助你设计？
35. 设计走查时，你最关注哪些问题？发现偏差后如何推进修复？
36. 讲一次你与工程师发生分歧的经历：分歧是什么，最终如何达成一致？

## 五、跨职能伙伴：研究、运营、商业与客户成功（37–41）

37. 你如何与用户研究员协作，避免研究只停留在报告层面？
38. 当运营希望增加活动曝光、弹窗或引导，而你担心打扰用户时，你会怎么处理？
39. 你如何与数据分析师定义漏斗、实验和成功指标？
40. 如果销售或客户成功团队反馈“大客户需要一个定制功能”，你如何判断是否应该做？
41. 你如何向非设计背景的利益相关者解释设计价值并争取支持？

## 六、管理者 / 终面：影响力、领导力与组织视角（42–45）

42. 你如何在没有直接管理权的情况下推动跨团队项目？
43. 请讲一次你处理多个重要项目冲突的经历；你的排序依据是什么？
44. 你如何帮助初级设计师成长，或如何接受并运用反馈？
45. 如果入职后前 90 天由你负责规划，你会如何了解业务、建立关系并创造价值？

## 七、AI 相关：产品机会、体验设计与责任边界（46–50）

46. 你认为 AI 会如何改变 UX 设计师的工作流？哪些环节应由设计师保留最终判断？
47. 如果要为现有产品加入 AI 功能，你会怎样判断它是在解决真实问题，而不是为了“有 AI”？
48. 请设计一个 AI 助手的关键交互：用户如何输入意图、理解生成结果、修改结果并恢复控制权？
49. 当 AI 输出不准确、有偏见或不确定时，界面该如何传达可信度、风险和下一步行动？
50. 你如何设计 AI 产品的反馈闭环：收集哪些反馈、如何保护隐私、怎样用反馈提升体验？

## 使用建议

- 优先准备第 9、10、12、18、21、24、31、36、42、47、48 题；它们最容易展开为深度追问。
- 每个项目案例按“背景 → 问题 → 你的判断 → 行动 → 结果 → 反思”组织，明确说明个人贡献。
- AI 题不要只谈工具效率；同时说明用户控制权、可解释性、错误恢复、隐私与评估指标。

# 50 Common UX Designer Interview Questions (English)

Natural, conversational versions for UX, product design, and interaction design interviews. Each question is 88 characters or fewer, including spaces and punctuation.

## 1. HR / Recruiting: Motivation, experience, and career choices (1–8)

1. Could you introduce yourself and highlight a project that shows your design strengths?
2. Why did you choose UX or product design over visual design or product management?
3. Why are you leaving your current or most recent role?
4. Why this company, product, and role?
5. Where do you see your design career in the next three years?
6. What's your biggest strength as a designer, and how has it created value?
7. Tell me about a decision that did not go well. What did you learn?
8. How do you manage priorities when requirements keep changing?

## 2. Design leaders / senior designers: Project depth and design judgment (9–20)

9. Walk me through a project you led: context, goal, process, outcome, and your role.
10. How did you define the problem and validate it was worth solving?
11. What research did you conduct, and how did it change your design?
12. How do you make trade-offs across user needs, business goals, and tech limits?
13. Tell me about a time you changed direction. What evidence changed your mind?
14. How do you structure information architecture and user flows for complex products?
15. How do you separate what users say they want from what they actually need?
16. When the brief is vague, where do you start?
17. How do you know an interface is clear, usable, and well structured?
18. Tell me how you measured a design's impact. What were the metrics and results?
19. What do you do when testing or live data does not support your design?
20. How do you turn a one-off design into reusable components or guidelines?

## 3. Product managers: Business understanding, strategy, and collaboration (21–29)

21. If a PM says “improve conversion,” how do you break down the problem?
22. What user value and business goal matter most for this product right now?
23. Tell me about an opportunity you spotted before anyone asked for it.
24. How do you handle a disagreement with a PM on priorities or direction?
25. How do you turn research insights into testable product hypotheses?
26. How would you design and evaluate an MVP for a new feature?
27. How do you balance short-term conversion with long-term user experience?
28. How do you prevent the team from building features that miss the real problem?
29. If a key metric stalls after launch, what do you investigate first?

## 4. Engineers / technical leads: Feasibility and engineering collaboration (30–36)

30. When do you bring engineers into the design process, and why?
31. Tell me about a design you changed because of technical constraints.
32. How do you hand off designs so the build stays true to the intended experience?
33. If a solution is too complex for the timeline, how would you phase it?
34. How do front-end, analytics, or accessibility knowledge shape your design work?
35. What do you check in design QA, and how do you get issues fixed?
36. Tell me about a disagreement with an engineer. How did you align?

## 5. Cross-functional partners: Research, operations, commercial, and customer success (37–41)

37. How do you make sure research leads to action, not just a report?
38. How would you balance operations requests with a low-interruption user experience?
39. How do you define funnels, experiments, and success metrics with analysts?
40. How would you decide whether to build a custom feature for a major client?
41. How do you explain design value to stakeholders without a design background?

## 6. Managers / final interviewers: Influence, leadership, and organizational perspective (42–45)

42. How do you drive a cross-functional project without direct authority?
43. Tell me how you prioritized when several important projects competed for attention.
44. How do you help junior designers grow, or apply feedback yourself?
45. How would you use your first 90 days to learn, build relationships, and add value?

## 7. AI: Product opportunity, interaction design, and responsible boundaries (46–50)

46. How will AI change UX design, and where should designers keep final judgment?
47. How do you tell if an AI feature solves a real user problem?
48. How would users prompt, review, edit, and control an AI assistant?
49. How should the UI handle AI output that is wrong, biased, or uncertain?
50. How would you collect AI feedback, protect privacy, and improve the experience?
