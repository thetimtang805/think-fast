# UX 设计师高频面试题 50 道（含跨角色与 AI 方向）

适用对象：产品设计师、UX/UI 设计师、交互设计师。题目按常见面试官角色整理；同一道主问题通常会被连续追问，准备时建议为每题配一个真实项目案例。

## 一、HR / 招聘方：动机、经历与职业选择（1–8）

1. 请用 3 分钟介绍一下你自己，以及最能代表你设计能力的经历。
2. 为什么选择 UX / 产品设计，而不是纯视觉设计或产品经理？
3. 你为什么想离开现在（或上一份）工作？
4. 为什么选择我们公司、这个产品和这个岗位？
5. 你的职业规划是什么？未来 3 年希望成长为怎样的设计师？
6. 你认为自己最突出的优势是什么？它如何在项目中产生价值？
7. 说一个你做得不够好的项目或决定；后来你怎样改进？
8. 当工作节奏快、需求频繁变化时，你如何管理压力和优先级？

## 二、设计负责人 / 资深设计师：项目深挖与专业判断（9–20）

9. 请完整讲一个你主导的项目：背景、目标、过程、结果和你的具体贡献分别是什么？
10. 在这个项目里，最初的问题是如何定义的？你如何确认它值得解决？
11. 你做了哪些用户研究？研究结论如何真正改变了设计决策？
12. 当用户需求、业务目标和技术成本冲突时，你如何做取舍？
13. 请讲一次你推翻自己初始设计方案的经历，是什么证据让你改变了判断？
14. 你如何为复杂流程建立信息架构和任务流？
15. 你怎样区分“用户说想要的”与“用户真正需要的”？
16. 面对模糊需求，你通常从哪里开始推进？
17. 你如何判断一个界面是清晰、易用且有层级的？
18. 讲一个你用数据衡量设计效果的案例：指标是什么、如何归因、结果如何？
19. 当可用性测试或线上数据不支持你的方案时，你会怎么做？
20. 你如何把一个一次性的设计方案沉淀为组件、规范或设计系统？

## 三、产品经理：业务理解、策略与协作（21–29）

21. 如果 PM 只给你一句“把转化率做高”，你会如何拆解问题并开始设计？
22. 你如何理解这个产品当前最关键的用户价值和商业目标？
23. 请讲一次你主动发现机会、而不是接到需求后才开始设计的经历。
24. 当你不同意 PM 的需求优先级或方案时，你如何表达并推动决策？
25. 你如何将研究洞察转化成可执行、可验证的产品假设？
26. 你会如何设计并评估一个新功能从 0 到 1 的 MVP？
27. 面对短期转化目标与长期用户体验，你会如何选择？
28. 你如何参与需求评审，避免团队做出“看似合理但不解决问题”的功能？
29. 如果上线后核心指标没有改善，你会优先排查哪些可能性？

## 四、工程师 / 技术负责人：可实现性与研发协作（30–36）

30. 你会在什么阶段邀请工程师参与设计？为什么？
31. 请讲一次因技术限制而改方案的经历：你如何找到体验与成本之间的平衡？
32. 你如何向工程师交付设计，确保实现效果和设计意图一致？
33. 当研发认为方案过于复杂、排期不可接受时，你会如何拆分或降级？
34. 你对前端、平台能力、数据埋点或无障碍分别了解多少？这些知识如何帮助你设计？
35. 设计走查时，你最关注哪些问题？发现偏差后如何推进修复？
36. 讲一次你与工程师发生分歧的经历：分歧是什么，最终如何达成一致？

## 五、跨职能伙伴：研究、运营、商业与客户成功（37–41）

37. 你如何与用户研究员协作，避免研究只停留在报告层面？
38. 当运营希望增加活动曝光、弹窗或引导，而你担心打扰用户时，你会怎么处理？
39. 你如何与数据分析师定义漏斗、实验和成功指标？
40. 如果销售或客户成功团队反馈“大客户需要一个定制功能”，你如何判断是否应该做？
41. 你如何向非设计背景的利益相关者解释设计价值并争取支持？

## 六、管理者 / 终面：影响力、领导力与组织视角（42–45）

42. 你如何在没有直接管理权的情况下推动跨团队项目？
43. 请讲一次你处理多个重要项目冲突的经历；你的排序依据是什么？
44. 你如何帮助初级设计师成长，或如何接受并运用反馈？
45. 如果入职后前 90 天由你负责规划，你会如何了解业务、建立关系并创造价值？

## 七、AI 相关：产品机会、体验设计与责任边界（46–50）

46. 你认为 AI 会如何改变 UX 设计师的工作流？哪些环节应由设计师保留最终判断？
47. 如果要为现有产品加入 AI 功能，你会怎样判断它是在解决真实问题，而不是为了“有 AI”？
48. 请设计一个 AI 助手的关键交互：用户如何输入意图、理解生成结果、修改结果并恢复控制权？
49. 当 AI 输出不准确、有偏见或不确定时，界面该如何传达可信度、风险和下一步行动？
50. 你如何设计 AI 产品的反馈闭环：收集哪些反馈、如何保护隐私、怎样用反馈提升体验？

## 使用建议

- 优先准备第 9、10、12、18、21、24、31、36、42、47、48 题；它们最容易展开为深度追问。
- 每个项目案例按“背景 → 问题 → 你的判断 → 行动 → 结果 → 反思”组织，明确说明个人贡献。
- AI 题不要只谈工具效率；同时说明用户控制权、可解释性、错误恢复、隐私与评估指标。

# 50 Common UX Designer Interview Questions (English)

Natural, conversational versions for UX, product design, and interaction design interviews. The questions below match the Chinese set one-to-one.

## 1. HR / Recruiting: Motivation, experience, and career choices (1–8)

1. Could you give me a three-minute introduction and highlight the experience that best represents your design strengths?
2. Why did you choose UX or product design instead of visual design or product management?
3. Why are you looking to leave your current—or most recent—role?
4. Why this company, this product, and this role?
5. What does your career path look like, and what kind of designer do you hope to become over the next three years?
6. What would you say is your biggest strength as a designer, and how has it created value in a project?
7. Tell me about a project or decision that did not go as well as you hoped. What did you do differently afterward?
8. How do you manage priorities and pressure when the pace is fast and requirements keep changing?

## 2. Design leaders / senior designers: Project depth and design judgment (9–20)

9. Walk me through a project you led end to end: the context, goal, process, outcome, and your specific contribution.
10. How did you define the original problem in that project, and how did you validate that it was worth solving?
11. What user research did you conduct, and how did the findings actually change your design decisions?
12. How do you make trade-offs when user needs, business goals, and technical constraints conflict?
13. Tell me about a time you changed your initial design direction. What evidence changed your mind?
14. How do you structure the information architecture and user flow for a complex experience?
15. How do you tell the difference between what users say they want and what they actually need?
16. When a brief is vague, where do you usually start?
17. How do you decide whether an interface is clear, easy to use, and well structured?
18. Tell me about a time you used data to measure design impact. What metrics did you use, how did you attribute the result, and what happened?
19. What do you do when usability testing or live data does not support your design direction?
20. How do you turn a one-off solution into reusable components, guidelines, or part of a design system?

## 3. Product managers: Business understanding, strategy, and collaboration (21–29)

21. If a PM only says, “Improve conversion,” how would you break the problem down and get started?
22. What do you see as this product’s most important user value and business goal right now?
23. Tell me about a time you spotted an opportunity proactively rather than waiting for a request.
24. When you disagree with a PM on priorities or direction, how do you communicate your point of view and move the decision forward?
25. How do you turn research insights into product hypotheses that are actionable and testable?
26. How would you design and evaluate an MVP for a new feature from zero to one?
27. How do you balance a short-term conversion goal with the long-term user experience?
28. How do you contribute in requirement reviews to prevent the team from building features that sound reasonable but do not solve the real problem?
29. If a key metric does not improve after launch, what would you investigate first?

## 4. Engineers / technical leads: Feasibility and engineering collaboration (30–36)

30. At what point do you bring engineers into the design process, and why?
31. Tell me about a time technical constraints forced you to change a design. How did you balance the experience with implementation cost?
32. How do you hand designs off to engineers so the final build stays true to the intended experience?
33. If engineering says a solution is too complex or cannot fit the timeline, how would you simplify or phase it?
34. How familiar are you with front-end development, platform capabilities, analytics instrumentation, or accessibility? How does that knowledge influence your design work?
35. What do you look for in a design QA review, and how do you get implementation issues resolved?
36. Tell me about a disagreement you had with an engineer. What was the disagreement, and how did you reach alignment?

## 5. Cross-functional partners: Research, operations, commercial, and customer success (37–41)

37. How do you partner with user researchers to make sure research leads to action, not just a report?
38. If an operations team wants more promotions, pop-ups, or prompts but you are concerned about interrupting users, how would you handle it?
39. How do you work with data analysts to define funnels, experiments, and success metrics?
40. If sales or customer success says a major client needs a custom feature, how would you decide whether to build it?
41. How do you explain the value of design and earn support from stakeholders without a design background?

## 6. Managers / final interviewers: Influence, leadership, and organizational perspective (42–45)

42. How do you drive a cross-functional project when you do not have direct authority?
43. Tell me about a time several important projects competed for attention. How did you prioritize them?
44. How do you help junior designers grow, or how do you seek out and apply feedback yourself?
45. If you joined the team, how would you use your first 90 days to learn the business, build relationships, and create value?

## 7. AI: Product opportunity, interaction design, and responsible boundaries (46–50)

46. How do you think AI will change a UX designer’s workflow, and where should designers retain final judgment?
47. If you were adding AI to an existing product, how would you determine whether it solves a real user problem rather than just adding AI for its own sake?
48. Design a key interaction for an AI assistant: how would users express intent, understand the output, edit it, and stay in control?
49. When an AI output is inaccurate, biased, or uncertain, how should the interface communicate confidence, risk, and the user’s next step?
50. How would you design an AI feedback loop: what feedback would you collect, how would you protect privacy, and how would you use it to improve the experience?
